## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment — mixed:** The site has real authored moments. The SixBars component translates the logo mark into a structural element using correct proportional bar heights from the actual brand asset — this is brand DNA in code, not decoration. The WhatWeDo interactive service list is the clearest proof of authorship: hover triggers a sticky image crossfade, description update, and directional arrow shift in a pattern clearly designed for the browsing behaviour of a print buyer comparing services. The fluid `clamp()` h1 at 9rem max with 0.86 line-height and −0.03em tracking is a deliberate typographic stance.

But the macro structure is recognisably "premium agency template" territory: Hero → Brand Statement → Services List → Quality Image Band → Portfolio Grid → Case Study → Why Us 4-grid → Process 4-grid → Industries → CTA. This sequence is the blueprint of every Framer/Webflow minimal template from the past three years. Three structurally identical numbered gap-px 4-column card grids (WhyFunoon, ProcessSteps, About Principles) appear across the site, erasing the specificity each new instance was meant to contribute. The visual system could be transplanted to a premium catering company or architecture firm with only the service list and photography changed.

**Deterministic scan:** CLI detector returned exit code 0 — zero findings on both `app/` and `components/` directories. The palette compliance pass confirmed every hex value in `globals.css` falls within the declared monochrome system. No off-palette colours, no missing alt text, no aria-label gaps in the automated scan. The detector's clean result confirms the visual system is rigorous; the issues that matter here are structural and editorial, outside what a style detector catches.

**Browser visualization:** Pages render correctly at localhost:3000. Monochrome discipline is intact visually. No broken images, no layout overflow at desktop. Key issues surfaced by source inspection rather than visual scan.

## Overall Impression

The typographic system and brand motif work. The WhatWeDo service list is genuinely excellent. Everything else is fighting the same two problems: placeholder content left live where it does the most damage (the case study, the business hours), and a headline bank with too few distinct lines spread across too many pages. A site positioning on precision and craft that repeats its own tagline four times does not yet prove precision or craft — it proves the design system is solid but the content layer is unfinished. Fix the copy and remove the placeholder sections before launch.

## What's Working

**1. WhatWeDo interactive service list.** The hover-driven image preview with live description update is the best-designed section on the site. It shows the product rather than listing it, mimics the physical experience of reviewing samples, and rewards attention with specificity. This is clearly authored for print buyers, not adapted from a template.

**2. Typographic execution.** The fluid clamp on h1 (3.25rem → 9rem), tight negative tracking (−0.03em), and 0.86 line-height on display headings produce a display type with real character. The contrast between Archivo Bold at scale and DM Sans body at text-sm with relaxed leading is well-calibrated. This is typographic skill, not just picking two font pairings.

**3. SixBars as structural anchor.** The component appears at the hero, quality band, enquiry CTA, quote page hero, and footer — always contextually placed (before a headline or beside a label), always aria-hidden, always sized to its context. It functions as a section-entry signal and brand DNA simultaneously, without becoming noise. Correct proportional heights (0.65, 0.85, 1.0, 0.75, 0.52, 0.38) match the actual logo mark.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Duplicate headline copy — 4 instances of the same tagline

**Why it matters:** "Let's make something worth printing." appears verbatim on the Quote page hero, About page CTA, EnquiryCTA section, and the footer. "The print businesses need to show up well." appears on both the Services page h1 and the WhatWeDo section h2. A brand positioning on precision and craft cannot produce the same headline four times. Visitors who browse more than two pages notice immediately. The premium voice collapses when it repeats itself.

**Fix:** Each page hero needs a page-specific headline. The Quote page should read something like "Tell us about your next print project." The About page CTA should reference the craft ethos specifically, not repeat the general tagline. The EnquiryCTA and footer CTA should differ from each other — they appear back-to-back on every page.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify` — rewrite hero and CTA headlines for each page with distinct, page-specific framings

---

### [P0] `[BUSINESS HOURS]` placeholder renders literally on the contact page

**Why it matters:** The contact page is the highest-stakes trust page on the site. A visible unresolved template variable tells buyers the site was shipped before it was finished. In B2B vendor comparison, this detail alone can disqualify. Both the visual browser check and source grep confirm this string renders to the user.

**Fix:** Populate `site.hours.time` in `lib/site.ts` with the actual operating hours string (e.g., "9:00 AM – 6:00 PM, Sunday – Thursday"). If hours are genuinely unknown at launch, use "Contact us for availability" rather than the unresolved variable.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify` — fix `lib/site.ts` hours field; audit all other site config fields for unresolved placeholders

---

### [P1] "Example Project" case study is live on the homepage

**Why it matters:** The CaseStudyFeature section shows "Client: Example Project" to every visitor. For a site with no client logos, no testimonials, and placeholder work photography, this is the only named proof-of-work on the homepage — and it declares itself fictional. The trust built by the hero and WhatWeDo sections erodes at exactly the moment the visitor is looking for evidence. Assessment B confirmed the string is in source: `components/home/case-study-feature.tsx`.

**Fix:** Remove the CaseStudyFeature section from `app/page.tsx` entirely until a real project is available. Replace with extended whitespace before the WhyFunoon section, or expand the FeaturedWork grid to two rows.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill` — remove CaseStudyFeature from homepage; adjust surrounding section spacing

---

### [P1] Hero CTA hierarchy inverts the conversion goal

**Why it matters:** The primary filled button says "View Our Work." The secondary outline button says "Request a Quote." The site's sole conversion goal is enquiry. Routing first visitors into placeholder portfolio photography before they have any reason to trust the company is backwards: visitors who click the primary CTA encounter the weakest part of the site.

**Fix:** Swap button order and variants in `components/home/hero.tsx`: make "Request a Quote" the primary `variant="primary"` button, "View Our Work" the secondary `variant="outlineLight"`. When real photography is available, reconsider the hierarchy.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout` — swap hero CTA order and variants

---

### [P1] `focus:outline-none` on form inputs removes the global keyboard focus ring (WCAG 2.4.11)

**Why it matters:** Assessment B's source scan found that the `fieldBase` class in `components/quote-form.tsx:12` includes `focus:outline-none`. Tailwind's `focus:` prefix generates a `:focus` rule. Because `:focus` is more specific than `:focus-visible`, this suppresses the global 2px solid #000 ring defined in `globals.css` for all focused form controls. The only remaining keyboard indicator is a border-colour change — low contrast, no ring, likely fails WCAG 2.4.11 Focus Appearance. This is invisible to visual QA because mouse users never trigger the focus ring.

**Fix:** In `components/quote-form.tsx`, replace `focus:outline-none` with `focus-visible:outline-none focus:border-ink` so the global ring is preserved for keyboard navigation while still cancelling the browser outline for pointer focus.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit` — fix WCAG 2.4.11 violation in quote-form.tsx:12; audit all other form inputs for the same pattern

## Minor Observations

- The `↗` arrow used on service list items and service cards suggests top-right movement rather than "click to see more." A `→` would be more directionally honest for a link that opens a detail page.
- The mobile menu overlay uses `opacity-0 + pointer-events-none` when closed but no `aria-hidden` or `inert`. Screen reader users can tab into hidden nav links without visually opening the menu. Fix: add `aria-hidden={!open}` to the overlay div (`components/site-header.tsx:96`).
- No skip-to-content link exists anywhere. Keyboard users must tab through the full navigation on every page load before reaching main content.
- The server action in `app/actions.ts:43` logs the full submitted payload — name, email, phone, company — to stdout with `console.log`. In a production deployment with log aggregation, this is unintentional PII leakage. Remove before going live.
- Form implicit label wrapping (`<label>` wrapping `<input>` with label text in a nested `<span>`) works in modern assistive tech but some older screen readers associate only direct text children. Add `id` to each input and `htmlFor` to each `<label>` for unambiguous association.
- The industries section image cards have a `group-hover:scale-105` animation but are not wrapped in a link or button — they appear interactive but are not. Remove the hover effect or add the link.
- The quality-band section and About page hero both use `/work/quality-detail.png`. When real photography is available, these should use distinct images.
- Footer copyright hardcodes the year (`© 2026 Funoon Print Co.`) — confirm this is dynamically generated or mark for annual update.
- The page title format is "About | Funoon Print Co." — consider reversing to "Funoon Print Co. | About" for brand-first tab labelling.

## Questions to Consider

- If a procurement manager must summarise Funoon in one sentence to their director after visiting this site, what would they write? Is there a single memorable, specific claim that survives that compression — or does "premium print company" cover everything the site says?
- WhatsApp is the dominant B2B communication channel in the Gulf. The site mentions it only in sidebar copy. Should a persistent fixed WhatsApp button outrank the quote form as the primary enquiry mechanism for this market?
- The portfolio is entirely placeholder. Does the site launch before or after real photography is available? If before, which sections should be deferred rather than shown with placeholder content that actively undermines the premium claim?
- The site is entirely in English with no Arabic presence. For a Sharjah-based company serving UAE corporate clients, should there be any Arabic signal — even the city name in script, or a bilingual footer note — to register cultural alignment with the primary market?
- The monochrome system creates sophistication but risks coldness in a market where warmth and relationship are B2B expectations in the Gulf. Is there a micro-interaction, copy tone, or layout change that adds warmth without breaking the visual discipline?
